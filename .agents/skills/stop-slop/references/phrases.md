# Phrases to Remove

Eliminate the following phrases, words, and transitions from your explanations, documentation, and user communication to ensure a highly dense, professional tone.

---

## 1. Conversational Boilerplate & Padding

AI agents frequently use friendly filler sentences before or after performing tasks. **Cut these completely.** Start directly with the content.

- "Certainly! I can help with that."
- "Sure, here is the..."
- "I'd be happy to assist you in..."
- "Let's take a look at..."
- "I hope this helps!"
- "Please let me know if you have any questions or need further clarification."
- "If you need anything else, just ask."
- "As requested,"
- "Here is the result of..."

---

## 2. Robotic Transitions & Meta-Commentary

Avoid announcing your plan or commenting on your own writing structure. Let the actions and documentation speak for themselves.

- "I will now proceed to..."
- "Let's first list the steps..."
- "In this section, we will unpack..."
- "Now, let me explain..."
- "It is worth noting that..."
- "As we can see,"
- "As mentioned above,"
- "Let's dive into..."

---

## 3. Throat-Clearing Openers

These phrases delay the message. State the assertion or detail immediately.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]"
- "Here's why [X] matters:"
- "The truth is,"
- "Let me be clear,"
- "The uncomfortable truth is,"
- "It turns out that..."

---

## 4. Empty Adverbs & Intensifiers

Kill all adverbs (especially `-ly` words). They are often used as "emphasis crutches" to make a simple task sound impressive.

- **"perfectly"** (e.g., "perfectly aligned", "perfectly suited")
- **"flawlessly"** (e.g., "compiled flawlessly")
- **"successfully"** (e.g., "successfully modified the file")
- **"effortlessly"** (e.g., "effortlessly manages state")
- **"easily"** (e.g., "can easily be customized")
- **"simply"** (e.g., "simply run the command")
- **"really" / "just" / "literally"**
- **"genuinely" / "honestly"**
- **"fundamentally" / "inherently"**
- **"crucially" / "importantly" / "interestingly"**

---

## 5. Vague Declaratives & Manufactured Hype

Avoid announcing importance or difficulty without backing it up with facts or code.

- "The implications are significant." -> State the exact implication.
- "This is a game-changer." -> State why it is useful.
- "The stakes are high." -> Name the exact risk.
- "This is a very powerful feature." -> Show the capability.
- "At its core," / "At the end of the day," -> Redundant.
